When people start a diet, a lot of them cut their training in half. Less food, worse recovery, so less work. Sounds logical.

A recent study pushes back on that. Advanced lifters ate in a 40% deficit, with plenty of protein, for four weeks. Half trained 12 sets a week per muscle. The other half trained 30.

On DXA scans there was no statistically significant difference in body composition. Both groups lost fat and kept their lean mass. Strength is where they differed. The 30-set group added 15 to 27% on the strength tests, the 12-set group 7 to 14%.

Now the small print. It's a preprint, still under review. Four weeks and a DXA scan can't tell real muscle growth from the body simply learning the movement. A 40% cut is aggressive, and these people already lifted. For me it's a signal worth watching. I wouldn't build a rule on it yet.

What I take from it: don't cut sets on a diet just out of habit. Let the plan decide that.
