It's tempting to cut your training when a diet starts. Less food, worse recovery, so less work. Sounds logical.

A recent study pushes back on that. Advanced lifters ate in a large deficit, with plenty of protein, for a few weeks. Half trained a moderate number of sets per muscle each week. The other half trained far more.

On DXA scans there was no statistically significant difference in body composition. Both groups lost fat and kept their lean mass. Strength is where they differed. The high-volume group gained more on the strength tests.

Now the small print. It's a preprint, still under review. In a few weeks, most of the strength gain is likely the nervous system getting better at the lifts. And DXA can't fully separate muscle from water and glycogen. A deficit that deep is aggressive, and these people already lifted. For me it's a signal worth watching. I wouldn't build a rule on it yet.

What I take from it: don't cut sets on a diet just out of habit. Let the plan decide that.
