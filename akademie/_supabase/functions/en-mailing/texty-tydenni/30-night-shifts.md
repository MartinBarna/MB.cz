This one is for you if you work nights or a rotating roster. If that's not you, feel free to skip it.

Shift work is clearly linked to weight gain. A 2018 meta-analysis put the odds of overweight or obesity about 23% higher with night shift work. A 2021 meta-analysis found shift work significantly linked to metabolic syndrome. This isn't a willpower problem.

What happens: a disrupted body clock throws off hunger and fullness signals. Your body isn't sure when it has had enough. Add worse sleep, a pull toward something fast, and whatever food is on offer at 3 a.m.

The physics still holds on a night shift. Calories don't disappear. What shifts break is your ability to stick with the plan. So build the plan for days that don't go by the book.

- Put the main meal before the shift or after it, not in the middle of the night.
- Bring food from home, so 3 a.m. doesn't choose for you.
- Make sleep the priority. Don't answer a bad week by cutting calories harder. Less sleep means more hunger.
- If your workplace allows it, a planned 20- to 30-minute nap mid-shift can help alertness.
