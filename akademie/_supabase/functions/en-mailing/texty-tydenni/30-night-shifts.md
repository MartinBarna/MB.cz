This one is for you if you work nights or a rotating roster. If that's not you, feel free to skip it.

Shift work has a measurable effect. A 2018 meta-analysis put obesity risk about 23% higher in shift workers. A 2021 meta-analysis put the risk of metabolic syndrome about 30% higher. It's not your weak will.

What happens: a knocked-about body clock scrambles hunger and fullness. Your body isn't sure when it has had enough. Add worse sleep, a pull toward something fast, and whatever food is on offer at 3 a.m.

The physics still holds on a night shift. Calories don't disappear. What shifts break is your ability to stick with the plan. So the setup has to count on a day that doesn't look like a textbook.

- Put the main meal before the shift or after it, not in the middle of the night.
- Bring food from home, so 3 a.m. doesn't choose for you.
- Make sleep the priority. Don't answer a bad week by cutting calories harder. Less sleep means more hunger.
- A planned nap of 20 to 30 minutes in the middle of the shift helps energy and cravings.
