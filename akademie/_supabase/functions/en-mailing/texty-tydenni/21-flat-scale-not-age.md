The weekly average has sat still, and the first story is age. Forty, forty-five, fifty. The metabolism must have switched off.

The biggest set of measurements we have says otherwise. Daily energy use, measured with doubly labeled water, covers people from 8 days old to 95 years (Pontzer, 2021). Per kg of fat-free mass, muscle and organs, energy use stays stable from 20 to 60. It starts to fall only later. Age 40 isn't a break point in that data.

What does change is the muscle itself. An MRI study of 468 men and women, aged 18 to 88, found the share of muscle in body weight falling from the 20s. A clear drop in the amount of muscle showed up toward the late 40s, mostly in the lower half of the body (Janssen, 2000). Less muscle, less energy use. The cause is often what left the day: stairs, longer walks, the car and the desk. Not a switch thrown on a birthday.

So a flat month isn't a reason to crash the food to 1,200 kcal. After 40 that trade is worse than at 20. More muscle goes with it, and at 50 muscle comes back more slowly than it left.

An earlier email already has the small move when two honest weeks are flat. This one is why you don't throw the mild plan out because a birthday arrived. Keep the muscle. Keep the walking. Judge the trend over months, not over two weeks.
