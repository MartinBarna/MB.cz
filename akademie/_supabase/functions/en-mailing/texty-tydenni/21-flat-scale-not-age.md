The weekly average has sat still, and the first story is age. Forty, forty-five, fifty. The metabolism must have switched off.

A large doubly labeled water dataset says otherwise. It covers daily energy use in people from 8 days old to 95 years (Pontzer, 2021). Per kg of fat-free mass (muscle, organs, bone, water), energy use stays stable from 20 to 60. It starts to fall only later. Age 40 isn't a break point in that data.

What does change is the muscle itself. An MRI study of 468 men and women, aged 18 to 88, found the share of muscle in body weight falling from the 20s. A clear drop in the amount of muscle showed up toward the late 40s, mostly in the lower half of the body (Janssen, 2000). Less muscle, less energy use. The cause is often how the day changed: fewer stairs, shorter walks, more time in the car and at the desk.

So a flat month isn't a reason to crash the food to 1,200 kcal. After 40 that trade is worse than at 20. A crash diet tends to cost more muscle, and rebuilding it gets slower with age.

An earlier email already has the small move when two honest weeks are flat. This one is why you don't throw the mild plan out because a birthday arrived. Keep the muscle and keep walking. The two-week check is for small tweaks. Whether the plan works shows over months.
