You don't have to weigh every meal for the rest of the year.

Counting is there to train your eye. For fourteen days, log everything. Cooking fat. Drinks. Bites while you cook. The things you tell yourself don't count. Very often there was no deficit at all, because those bits were missing from the log.

After two to three months, most clients estimate the normal meals well enough. They go back to the scale only for the foods they keep getting wrong. Those are usually sides, nuts and sauces. A piece of meat or fish is easier to see. Oil, nuts and the sauce on top are where the eye goes low.

There's a classic study on this blind spot. People said they ate under 1,200 kcal a day and still didn't lose weight. Measured energy use was within 5% of the predicted number, so their metabolism wasn't the problem. They underreported intake by 47% on average, and they overreported activity by 51%. Nobody was lying on purpose. They estimated.

So the scale is a teacher for fourteen days. After that, weigh the foods that still fool you, and estimate the rest.
