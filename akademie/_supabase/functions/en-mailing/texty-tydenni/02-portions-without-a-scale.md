You don't have to weigh every meal for the rest of the year.

Counting is there to train your eye. For fourteen days, log everything. Cooking fat. Drinks. Bites while you cook. The things you tell yourself don't count. With my clients, very often the deficit was never there. Those bits just didn't make it into the log.

Fourteen days teaches you the basics. After two to three months of checking now and then, most of my clients estimate normal meals well enough. They go back to the scale only for the foods they keep getting wrong. Those are usually oil, nuts and sauces. A piece of meat or fish is easy to judge. Fats are where the eye guesses low.

There's a well-known 1992 study on this blind spot. People who said they ate under 1,200 kcal a day and still weren't losing weight had their energy use measured. Low energy use was ruled out, so their metabolism wasn't the problem. They underreported intake by 47% on average, and they overreported activity by 51%. That's simply what estimating does, and it happens to all of us.

So the scale is your teacher for the first fourteen days. After that, weigh the foods that still fool you, and estimate the rest.
