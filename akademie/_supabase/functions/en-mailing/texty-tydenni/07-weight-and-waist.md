24 adults took part in a 6-week study. One group trained their abs 5 days a week, the other didn't, and everyone kept the same calories. The ab group did better on an endurance test. Abdominal fat didn't change in a significant way. The waist didn't either (Vispute, 2011).

The belly gets smaller when you lose fat across your whole body. For many people the belly is the last place to lose fat.

A realistic pace is about 0.3 to 0.7 kg of fat a week.

2 x 0.3 to 0.7 kg = 0.6 to 1.4 kg of fat in 14 days.

Over two weeks, that usually starts to show in the weekly average. That's not a flat belly yet. The belly takes longer. A promise of a flat belly in 14 days is counting on water loss, and that water comes back after a few days.

Measure the waist once a week, the same way each time, next to the weekly average on the scale. In the ab study, calories stayed the same, and so did the waist.

What helps the fat go: a mild deficit you can keep for months, protein around 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight, and lifting 2 to 4 times a week so the loss is fat and the muscle stays. Walking raises daily energy use without wearing you out. Short sleep and constant stress push hunger up and make the deficit harder to hold. The deficit still decides how much fat you lose.

If you suspect a hormone problem, or you take medication, talk to your doctor. Don't try to sort that out from an email. I'm a coach, not a doctor.
