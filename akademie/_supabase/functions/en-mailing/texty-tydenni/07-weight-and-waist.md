24 adults trained their abs 5 days a week for 6 weeks, and they kept the same calories. The muscles got stronger. Abdominal fat didn't change in a significant way. The waist didn't either (Vispute, 2011).

The belly gets smaller when fat goes down on the whole body. For many people the belly is the last place to lose fat.

A realistic pace is about 0.3 to 0.7 kg of fat a week.

2 x 0.3 to 0.7 kg = 0.6 to 1.4 kg of fat in 14 days.

You will notice that on the scale. It isn't a finished belly. A promise of a flat belly in 14 days is counting on water loss, and that water comes back after a few days.

Measure the waist once a week, the same way each time, next to the weekly average on the scale. In the sit-up study the waist stayed put because the calories stayed the same.

What helps the fat go: a mild deficit you can keep for months, protein around 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg, and lifting 2 to 4 times a week so the loss is fat and the muscle stays. Walking raises daily energy use without wearing you out. Too little sleep and constant stress can slow the loss and add to fat around the belly. They also make a deficit harder to hold.

If you suspect a hormone problem, or you take medication, talk to your doctor. Don't try to sort that out from an email. I'm a coach, not a doctor.
