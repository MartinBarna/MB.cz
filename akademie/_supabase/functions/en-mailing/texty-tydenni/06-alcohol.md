Beer with dinner, a glass of wine, a shot on Friday. The weight can still go down.

Fat loss still comes down to the calorie deficit for the week. Reviews of the research mostly don't find a clear link between light or moderate drinking and weight gain. A glass with dinner a few times in the week is one situation. Six beers on Friday is a different one.

Alcohol raises appetite, and you get less careful about the food in front of you. People eat more afterwards, and they don't eat less later to make up for it. A meta-analysis put that extra food at +343 kJ on average, about 82 kcal. That's an average across studies. On a real night out, the late pizza or kebab can easily cost more than the drinks did.

Two more things happen when you drink more, or more often:

Sleep gets worse. Alcohol suppresses REM sleep and breaks the night into pieces, mostly in the second half.

Muscle building gets slower when the dose is high. In one study, muscle protein building after training was lower with alcohol, even with protein in the meal. The dose was about 12 drinks. That's a heavy night out.

Health is a separate question from the scale. In 2023 the WHO said that no amount of alcohol is safe for health. Alcohol is a Group 1 carcinogen, the group with the strongest evidence that it causes cancer in humans, and it's addictive. A calorie deficit doesn't cancel that.

If you drink, count the drinks in the day. A few drinks in the week, counted, aren't what stops fat loss.
