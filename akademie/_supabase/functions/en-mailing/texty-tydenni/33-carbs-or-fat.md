Some people weigh every gram of carbs and fat, sure that the ratio decides their fat loss. Once calories and protein are set, the ratio barely moves the result.

Hall and Guo went through 32 tightly controlled studies that kept protein the same. The difference in fat loss between high-carb and low-carb came out at about 16 g a day. And that small edge went to the lower-fat diets.

Sacks followed 811 people for about two years on four different ratios. They lost about the same amount of weight.

Protein is a different story. Leidy found that at the same weight loss, higher protein kept almost half more muscle than lower protein.

So what to track: calories, protein, and fiber for fullness. Split carbs and fat by taste and by training. That's good news on the road. Pasta or a steak can both fit, as long as the day's total and the protein hold.

Two limits. Keep fat at roughly 25 to 35% of calories and don't live under 20% for long. And carbs fuel hard training.
