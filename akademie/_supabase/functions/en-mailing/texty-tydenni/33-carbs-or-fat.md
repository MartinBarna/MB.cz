Some people weigh every gram of carbs and fat, sure that the ratio decides their fat loss. Once calories and protein are set, the ratio barely moves the result.

Hall and Guo pooled 32 controlled feeding studies that swapped carbs for fat at equal calories. The lower-fat diets came out slightly ahead: people burned about 26 kcal a day more. That's a bite or two of food.

Sacks followed 811 people for about two years on four different ratios. They lost about the same amount of weight.

Protein is a different story. In a trial by Leidy and colleagues, at the same weight loss, the higher-protein group lost noticeably less lean mass.

What's worth tracking: calories, protein, and fiber for fullness. Split carbs and fat by taste and by training. That's good news on the road. Pasta and steak can both fit, as long as your daily total and protein stay on track.

Two caveats. Keep fat at roughly 25 to 35% of calories and don't stay below 20% for long. And if you train hard, eat enough carbs to fuel it.
