If I could check one thing in your food, it would be protein.

For most active people, 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight a day works well. At 80 kg:

80 x 1.6 = 128 g
80 x 2.2 = 176 g

That's about 130 to 175 g a day. If you're losing fat, aim at the higher end. You stay fuller, and hunger is lower.

A 2018 meta-analysis of strength training studies found that the benefit of extra protein for muscle flattens out at about 1.6 g per kg a day. I still use 1.6 to 2.2 g, with some margin on top. A 2012 meta-analysis of diet trials found that higher-protein diets gave a bit more fat loss and kept more lean mass than standard-protein diets.

You don't have to weigh every gram to get close. Put a protein food on the plate at breakfast, lunch and dinner.

Meat and fish. Eggs. Greek yogurt, skyr, quark, cheese. Beans, lentils, tofu. Protein powder on the day normal food doesn't reach the number.

If you've kidney disease, or any other medical condition, talk to your doctor before you change how much protein you eat. I'm a coach, not a doctor.
