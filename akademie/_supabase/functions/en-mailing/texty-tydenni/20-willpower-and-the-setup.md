"I'll be strict for a month, then eat normally again."

Look at the word normally. If normally means the way of eating that put the weight on, you get the same result. A diet with an end date doesn't hold. A change you can still keep in a year does.

Yo-yo dieting usually comes from a bad setup. Your character has little to do with it. Three things show up together: a deficit that's too big, muscle lost along the way, and a plan that was always going to stop.

Less muscle means you burn less at rest, including on the couch. Fat then comes back more easily when the old food returns.

There's a second push, and it isn't in your head. Sumithran and colleagues (2011) measured 50 people. Appetite hormones hadn't returned to normal even a year after the diet. Hunger stays louder. For most people you can't switch that off with willpower overnight.

A different study followed 14 people from an extreme weight-loss competition for six years. Resting metabolism stayed about 700 kcal a day lower. That's an extreme case, not a normal diet. Don't apply 700 kcal to yourself. On a mild deficit the picture I see is different: you want the old portions, and the hunger lasts longer than you hoped. The harder you cut, the harder the body defends the old weight.

The setup that lasts:

a mild deficit, 15 to 20% below your energy use
protein around 1.6 to 2 g per kg
lifting, so the body has a reason to keep muscle
after the diet, add calories back to maintenance step by step, and stay there for a while

Fat loss is a way of eating you can live with, long after the month is over.
