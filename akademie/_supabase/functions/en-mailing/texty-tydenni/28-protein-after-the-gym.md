A late session in the hotel gym, then a meeting. People ask me if the shake has to land the minute the last set ends. What matters more is how much protein you eat across the whole day.

On paper, two sources look like they disagree. Morton and colleagues (2018) found muscle gains level off around 1.6 g per kg of body weight on average. Refalo and Helms (2025) give a higher range. Their range is per kg of fat-free mass, though, not of what the scale shows. Convert it to body weight for a typical lifter and their lower edge lands close to the same place. At the low end the two meet. The top end is still debated.

On strength, Tagawa (2022) shows the curve climbing up to about 1.5 g per kg and flattening above that. Past that point, extra protein buys very little extra strength.

What I use with clients: 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of total body weight. I go toward 2.2 as insurance when the food log is rough (the error is often 20 to 50%) or the fat-loss phase is hard.

And protein isn't the first lever. Calories, training and sleep come first. Without those, extra protein mostly costs money.

On a trip, don't chase the shake. Get protein into whatever meals the day gives you.
