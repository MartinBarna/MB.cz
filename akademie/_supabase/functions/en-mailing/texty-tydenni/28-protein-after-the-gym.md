A late session in the hotel gym, then a meeting. People ask me if the shake has to land the minute the last set ends. What decides more is how much protein you eat across the whole day.

On paper, two numbers look like they disagree. Stuart Phillips says 1.6 g per kg of body weight covers 97 to 98% of people. Refalo and Helms (2025) say 1.9 to 3.2 g per kg. Their range is per kg of fat-free mass, though, not of what the scale shows. Convert it for a typical lifter at 18 to 28% body fat and their lower edge lands near 1.4 to 1.6 g per kg of body weight. At the low end the two meet. The top end is still debated.

On strength, Tagawa (2022) shows the curve climbing up to about 1.5 g per kg. Above that it flattens. More protein stops buying more strength in a straight line.

What I use with clients: 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of total body weight. I go toward 2.2 as insurance when the food log is rough (the error is often 20 to 50%) or the fat-loss phase is hard.

And protein isn't the first lever. Training, calories and sleep come first. Without those, protein is an expensive insurance policy.

So on a trip, don't chase the shake. Get protein into the meals you actually get that day.
