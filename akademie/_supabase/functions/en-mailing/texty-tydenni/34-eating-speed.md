A bag of pretzel sticks in the hotel room is gone in ten minutes. A plate of potatoes and chicken isn't. There's a measured reason for that.

Kevin Hall kept 20 adults on a ward for four weeks (2019). Two weeks on ultra-processed food, two weeks on unprocessed food. Both menus offered matched calories, macros, sugar, salt and fiber, and people could eat as much as they wanted. On the ultra-processed menu they ate 508 kcal a day more and gained 0.9 kg in those two weeks. On the unprocessed menu they lost 0.9 kg.

The physics worked as usual. People gained because they ate more, and the food let them eat more without noticing.

One likely lever is speed. Ciarán Forde and colleagues later highlighted the eating rate measured in that trial. Ultra-processed: 48 kcal a minute. Unprocessed: 31. Fullness takes a while to catch up with what you've eaten. Eat the same energy in about two-thirds of the time, and "I've had enough" shows up when you're already well ahead.

Texture seems to matter as much as the label. In another trial, harder versions of the same lunches were eaten more slowly, and people took in 26% less energy from them.

What you can do with any menu or minibar, without banning anything:

- Pick the harder, bulkier version of the same food. Boiled potatoes instead of mash. Whole nuts instead of a nut bar.
- Put the soft, dense food on a plate instead of eating it from the bag.
