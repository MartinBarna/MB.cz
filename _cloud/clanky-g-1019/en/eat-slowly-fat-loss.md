Lunch in seven minutes at the computer, dinner standing up, breakfast on the way to the car. When I ask clients how long a meal takes them, most of them pause, because they have never thought about it. Eating speed is one of the cheapest levers you have for fat loss. It bans nothing and costs nothing.

## What the studies say

A British meta-analysis of 22 experiments (Robinson et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2014, PMID 24847856) pooled studies where researchers changed people's eating rate: smaller bites, slower pace, more chewing. When people ate more slowly, they ate less. It worked regardless of how the slowing down was done. Results varied a lot between studies though.

An interesting detail: hunger at the end of the meal and three and a half hours later did not differ between fast and slow eating. People ate less and did not feel hungrier.

A Japanese meta-analysis of 23 studies (Ohkuma et al., Int J Obes 2015, PMID 26100137) looked from the other side. People who described themselves as fast eaters had on average a BMI 1.78 higher and about twice the odds of obesity compared with slow eaters. Careful here: this is observational, not an experiment. It does not prove that fast eating causes obesity, and people rated their own speed.

Put together, it makes sense. It takes a while for fullness signals from the stomach and gut to reach the brain. When the plate is gone in five minutes, you can easily eat an extra portion before you notice.

## Why we eat fast

In practice I see the same reasons again and again:

- **Eating in front of a screen.** Phone, laptop, TV. Your attention is elsewhere and your hand keeps going.
- **Big hunger.** Someone who has not eaten from morning until three in the afternoon does not eat, they inhale.
- **Food that needs no chewing.** Soft bread, porridge, smoothies, highly processed food. It goes down fast and fills you less.
- **Habit.** Anyone who grew up with siblings at one table knows what I mean.

## Five steps to slow down

1. **Sit down and put your phone away.** This alone makes the biggest difference for most people.
2. **Put your fork down between bites.** Sounds silly, works. You cannot load the next bite until you have finished chewing.
3. **Start with vegetables or soup.** Foods that need chewing and have volume slow you down naturally.
4. **Give a meal at least 15 to 20 minutes.** That number is a practical target from my coaching, not from the studies. If lunch takes you seven minutes, try twelve first.
5. **Do not wait until you are starving.** Regular meals with protein keep hunger under control, so you do not feel the need to wolf food down.

## What to expect

Slow eating alone will not make anyone lose weight, the calorie deficit still applies. It is a tool that helps you keep the deficit without feeling like you are starving. With clients who used to eat in a rush, I often see that once they slow down, they leave part of the portion on the plate without even noticing.

These are exactly the habits we build first in coaching: small, cheap, and they hold the whole plan together.

## FAQ

### How many minutes should a meal take?
The studies do not give a fixed number. As a practical target I recommend 15 to 20 minutes for a main meal. The key is to slow down compared with how you eat now.

### Does chewing every bite thirty times help?
More chewing reduced intake in experiments, but nobody keeps counting bites for long. Putting your fork down and eating without your phone work in a similar way and are easier.

### Does this apply to drinks too?
Sweet drinks and smoothies go down fast and fill you little. When losing fat, it is better to eat fruit than to drink it.
