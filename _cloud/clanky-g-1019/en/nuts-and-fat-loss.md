Clients see nuts as a healthy food, so they eat them without counting. A bag of cashews by the computer, a handful of almonds, then another handful. Others cut them completely because "they have so many calories". Both are a shame. Nuts are one of the foods that make sense when losing fat, but with nuts the portion matters more than almost anywhere else.

## What the studies say: nuts do not raise body weight

A Canadian and Spanish team (Nishi et al., Obes Rev 2021, PMID 34494363) pooled 86 randomised trials with almost 6,000 people and six large cohort studies with more than half a million people. The results:

- In the randomised trials, nuts did not increase body weight. The difference was 0.09 kg, practically zero, with high certainty of evidence.
- In the cohort studies, people who ate nuts had a 7% lower risk of overweight or obesity.
- Higher nut intake was linked to a slight decrease in body weight and body fat.

The authors conclude that the worry that nuts add fat is not supported. A caveat on interpretation: in the studies, people mostly ate nuts instead of something else, not on top of everything.

## Your body does not absorb all of it

This is my favourite part about nuts. The US Department of Agriculture had 18 people eat a controlled diet with almonds and then measured exactly how much energy ended up in the stool (Novotny et al., Am J Clin Nutr 2012, PMID 22760558). Almonds delivered 4.6 kcal per gram instead of the 6.0 to 6.1 the food tables use. For a 28 g portion that is 129 kcal instead of 170. The tables overestimated almond energy by 32%.

The reason is the structure of the nut. The fat is locked inside cell walls that you do not crush completely when chewing, so part of the fat passes through unused. This does not apply equally to all nuts, and for nut butter the difference is smaller because grinding breaks the cells.

I would not adjust your log for it. Count nuts by the label, and if your real intake turns out a bit lower, even better.

## The catch: portion size

Now the less good news. Nuts have around 600 to 700 kcal per 100 g. A handful is 25 to 30 g and about 170 kcal. But from an open bag during a film you can easily eat 100 g, which is over 600 kcal, almost a whole lunch.

The worst offenders:

- **Salted and oil roasted nuts** and mixes with chocolate or yogurt coating. They disappear on their own.
- **Nut butter by the spoon from the jar.** One heaped spoon is easily 30 g and 180 kcal.
- **"Healthy" bars and granola**, where nuts are glued together with sugar.

## How to eat nuts while losing fat

- **Weigh a 25 to 30 g portion** and put the rest away. Never eat from the bag.
- **Plain nuts** instead of salted and coated ones.
- **Nuts as part of a meal.** On porridge, on yogurt, in a salad. They add flavour, crunch and satiety.
- **Weigh nut butter,** do not eyeball the spoon.
- **Nuts instead of another snack,** not on top of it.

If you want to know what your "handful" weighs, weigh it. Most clients are surprised.

You do not have to ban nuts, you just need to know how to work with them. That is exactly what I teach in coaching.

## FAQ

### How many nuts a day can I eat while losing fat?
In practice one portion of 25 to 30 g, a small handful, about 170 kcal. If it fits your calories, every day is fine.

### Which nuts are best for fat loss?
No nut makes you lose more than the others. Pick the ones you like, unsalted and uncoated. Portion matters, not the type.

### Is peanut butter the same as nuts?
Similar in calories, but it is easier to eat more of it and grinding removes part of the advantage of whole nuts. Buy one that contains only peanuts and weigh it.
