Two rolls with butter and ham for breakfast, a cheese sandwich as a snack, open sandwiches in the evening. In Czechia we eat a lot of bread, so when you decide to lose weight, cutting it sounds like the obvious first step. With clients it usually ends the same way: three weeks without bread, then a weekend that makes up for all of it. You can eat bread while losing fat. It helps to know where the problem is, because it is usually not the bread itself.

## Study: bread did not get in the way of fat loss

A Spanish trial (Loria Kohen et al., Nutr Hosp 2011, PMID 22072367) put 122 overweight women on a low calorie diet for 16 weeks. One group had bread in their meal plan, the other had it replaced with rice or pasta at the same calories. After 16 weeks, the bread group reported **higher satiety** ninety minutes after a test meal. The authors state plainly that the results go against the advice to remove bread from weight loss plans.

It is one study and the women rated satiety themselves on a scale. It matches what I see in practice though: people who keep bread in their plan stick to it longer and slip less.

## Which bread fills you more

In the well known satiety study (Holt et al., Eur J Clin Nutr 1995, PMID 7498104), 38 foods with the same 240 calories were compared, with white bread as the reference at 100%. The croissant came last at 47%. Wholegrain bread was clearly more filling than white. In general, foods with more fibre, water and protein filled people more, foods with more fat filled them less.

A large review in The Lancet (Reynolds et al. 2019, PMID 30638909) then showed that people with higher fibre and wholegrain intake have lower risk of heart disease, type 2 diabetes and colorectal cancer. In clinical trials, higher fibre intake was also linked to lower body weight. The best results came at 25 to 29 g of fibre a day.

For practice: wholegrain or rye bread with visible grains will fill you more than a white roll or a buttery croissant.

## Calories in bread, roughly

- **Czech roll (43 g):** about 120 kcal.
- **Slice of regular bread (50 g):** about 120 kcal.
- **Slice of wholegrain or rye bread (50 g):** about 110 to 130 kcal, with more fibre.
- **Croissant (60 g):** about 250 kcal, half of it from fat.
- **Sweet pastry:** 200 to 350 kcal depending on size and filling.

A roll on its own will not make you fat. Two rolls with 20 g of butter and full fat cheese are a 550 calorie breakfast that will not keep you full until ten.

## Where the problem really is

1. **What goes on the bread.** Butter, pâté, full fat cheese, salami, spreads. A thin layer of butter is about 10 g and 75 kcal, a "the way I like it" layer easily 25 g.
2. **Bread instead of protein.** Two rolls with jam are almost pure carbs. You are hungry again by ten.
3. **Bread on top of a meal.** Bread with soup, with goulash, with salad. Every slice is 120 kcal nobody counts.
4. **Sweet pastry as a snack.** A pastry with coffee has as many calories as a small lunch.

## How to eat bread while losing fat

- **Choose bread with fibre.** Rye, wholegrain, with seeds. It fills you better.
- **Add protein to every piece of bread.** Cottage cheese, quark spread, high meat ham, eggs, salmon.
- **Weigh your butter, at least for a few days.** You will see how much you really spread.
- **Add vegetables.** Tomato, pepper, cucumber. Volume for a few calories.
- **Count bread as a side.** If you have potatoes for lunch, you do not need extra bread with your soup.

You do not have to ban bread. I teach clients how to fit it into the day so it stays within calories and still keeps them full.

## FAQ

### Is a roll or bread better?
Calorie wise they are similar. Rye or wholegrain bread fills you more thanks to fibre. A white roll for breakfast is fine if you add protein and vegetables.

### Can I eat bread in the evening?
Yes. Meal timing has very little effect on fat loss. What matters is how much you eat over the whole day and what you put on the bread.

### Is gluten free bread better for fat loss?
Not for fat loss, it often has more calories and less fibre. A gluten free diet makes sense for people with coeliac disease or diagnosed gluten sensitivity.
