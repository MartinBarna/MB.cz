"I cut potatoes, they say you can't eat them when losing weight." I hear this in first consultations all the time. Then the client eats rice, pasta or nothing instead, and within a week is starving every evening. Potatoes are one of the best sides you can have for fat loss. It just depends on what you do with them.

## The most filling of 38 foods tested

In 1995, Australian researchers gave people 38 common foods, always a portion with the same 240 calories, and measured for two hours how full they felt (Holt et al., Eur J Clin Nutr, PMID 7498104). Then they offered food and recorded how much people ate.

The clear winner: boiled potatoes. They were more than three times as filling as white bread and about seven times as filling as a croissant, which came last. Foods that filled people up more had more water, fibre and protein. Foods with more fat filled them less.

The reason is simple. 100 g of boiled potatoes has about 80 calories. For the same 240 calories you get a full plate of potatoes, or a few bites of croissant. Your stomach notices volume, not calories.

## What diet studies with potatoes showed

A Californian study (Randolph et al., J Am Coll Nutr 2014, PMID 25302575) split 90 overweight people into three groups. Two groups were asked to cut 500 calories a day, the third changed nothing. **All three ate potatoes five to seven times a week.** After 12 weeks all groups lost a modest amount of weight with no difference between them. The authors put it soberly: weight loss followed energy intake and potatoes did not cause weight gain.

A newer controlled trial (Rebello et al., J Med Food 2022, PMID 36367708) fed 36 people with insulin resistance low energy density meals for eight weeks, with either potatoes or legumes. Both groups lost weight, the potato group 5.6% of body weight and the legume group 4.1%, and the difference was not significant. It was a small study with prepared meals. It does not make potatoes a miracle food, but it shows potatoes and fat loss go together fine.

## Cooking is what makes them fattening

The problem with potatoes starts in the kitchen. Roughly, per 200 g portion:

- **Boiled potatoes:** about 160 kcal.
- **Mashed potatoes with butter and milk:** easily double, depending on the fat.
- **Fries:** about 600 kcal, because they soak up oil.
- **Potato salad with mayo:** 400 to 500 kcal depending on the recipe.
- **Chips:** 200 g is over 1,000 kcal and barely fills you.

The potato itself is not to blame. The calories come from the fat you pour on it, fry it in or mix into it.

## How to eat potatoes while losing fat

- **Boiled, baked in the skin or steamed.** Oven baked with a teaspoon of oil and spices tastes like fries for a fraction of the calories.
- **Make mash with milk or a little cooking water** and add butter on the plate, so you know how much.
- **Potatoes instead of bread at dinner.** More filling for fewer calories.
- **Cold potatoes in a salad** with a yogurt dressing instead of mayo.
- **Daily is fine.** If they fit your calories, there is no reason to cut them.

This is flexible eating the way I teach it: no forbidden foods, just smart portions and preparation.

## FAQ

### Are potatoes better than rice or pasta?
For the same calories they fill you more because they contain more water. Boiled potatoes have about 80 kcal per 100 g, cooked rice about 130 and cooked pasta about 150. Rice and pasta are fine too. Portion size and what you add matter most.

### Can I eat potatoes in the evening?
Yes. Meal timing has very little effect on fat loss, total daily intake decides. Potatoes at dinner also fill you up well and help you get through the evening without snacking.

### Won't the starch make me gain weight?
Starch is a carbohydrate with 4 kcal per gram like any other. Potatoes have about 15 to 17 g of starch per 100 g, because the rest is mostly water. Weight gain comes from an energy surplus, not from one particular side dish.
