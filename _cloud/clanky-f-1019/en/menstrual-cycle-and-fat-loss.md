Three good weeks. Then the week before your period arrives, the scale shows a kilo more, your jeans feel tight and you want chocolate like you have not wanted it all month. This is exactly when clients message me that "it stopped working". It did not. You are looking at a number that says almost nothing about fat right now.

## The extra kilo before your period is water

Around your period, your body holds more water. A Canadian study followed 62 healthy women for a full year, 765 cycles in total, and each woman rated her fluid retention every day (White et al., Obstet Gynecol Int 2011, PMID 21845193). It peaked on the first day of bleeding and was lowest in the mid follicular phase, about a week after the period. Around ovulation it slowly rose again.

These were self-ratings, nobody stepped on a scale, so do not read exact grams into it. The pattern matches what I see with clients: higher numbers a few days before the period and at its start, then a drop, often below where you were before the spike.

You cannot gain a kilo of fat in three days. That would take roughly 7,700 extra calories. A kilo in three days is water, gut content and salt, and it leaves again.

## Do you burn more in the luteal phase? A little, and do not count on it

You will read online that you burn 300 calories more in the second half of your cycle. The data are more modest.

A meta-analysis of 26 studies with 318 women (Benton et al., PLoS One 2020, PMID 32658929) found a small effect towards higher resting metabolic rate in the luteal phase, after ovulation. In studies published since 2000 the difference shrank and was no longer statistically significant. The authors note the studies were small and measurements often imprecise.

A 2025 trial (Smith et al., Appetite, PMID 41005066) measured resting metabolism, hunger and food intake in 18 young women, in the lab and at home. Resting metabolism in the luteal phase averaged 104 calories a day higher, with so much spread that it was not significant. Hunger, cravings and food eaten did not differ between phases.

A small Chilean study (Maury-Sintjago et al., Nutrients 2022, PMID 35631136) saw lean women burn about 120 calories more at rest in the luteal phase, but eat about 317 more. One snapshot of 30 women, so I treat it as a signal and nothing more.

The practical takeaway: if there is a difference in burn, it is tens to low hundreds of calories. If cravings add more than that, the balance tips the other way.

## Cravings: plan for them

Some of my clients get strong cravings before their period, others barely notice. Studies disagree here, and the honest answer is that it depends on you. Track two or three cycles and note when cravings show up. Then you know which week to prepare for.

- **Plan your sweet thing in advance.** Two squares of dark chocolate logged in the morning beat half a bar eaten standing at the counter at night.
- **Protein and volume at every meal.** Greek yogurt, eggs, chicken, plus vegetables or soup. Hunger stays calmer.
- **Take that week at maintenance if you need to.** Your weekly and monthly balance matters more than one week. More on cravings [here](https://martinbarna.cz/en/blog/cravings-and-hunger.html).
- **Sleep.** Short sleep raises cravings any time of the month.

## Weigh yourself by cycle

Compare the same phase of your cycle, not week to week. The day before your period against the day before your period last month.

- Weigh every morning, decide on the weekly average.
- Change nothing in the week before and during your period.
- Measure your waist and take a photo every two weeks. They move less.

Why the scale jumps day to day in general, I explain [here](https://martinbarna.cz/en/blog/why-your-weight-fluctuates.html).

## Training: listen to yourself, keep the plan

A large review of 78 studies (McNulty et al., Sports Med 2020, PMID 32661839) found performance might be slightly lower in the early follicular phase, during your period. The authors called the effect trivial and the quality of evidence low, and recommend an individual approach over general rules.

My experience with clients is the same. If the first two days are rough, lower the weights or the sets, or go for a walk. If you feel fine, train as usual. Lifting is one of the best things women can do while losing fat, and it will not make you bulky, as I explain [here](https://martinbarna.cz/en/blog/will-lifting-make-women-bulky.html).

## When to see a doctor

If your period becomes irregular or stops while you are dieting, your body is telling you energy is too low. That belongs with your gynaecologist, even if the scale is going your way. The same goes for very painful or heavy periods and swelling that does not go away.

## FAQ

### How much can weight rise before a period?
With my clients I commonly see half a kilo to a kilo and a half, sometimes more. It is mostly water and it leaves after the period starts.

### Should I eat more in the luteal phase because I burn more?
I would not rely on it. The difference is small and uncertain in newer studies. If higher intake helps you that week, move to maintenance on purpose and log it.

### Does this apply on hormonal birth control?
Combined hormonal contraception dampens natural hormone swings, so weight swings are usually smaller. Comparing averages still applies. Questions about the contraception itself belong with your gynaecologist.
