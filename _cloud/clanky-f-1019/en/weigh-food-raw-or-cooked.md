A client sent me her food log: exactly 1,600 calories every day for three weeks, and the scale did not move. A perfect diary at first glance. At second glance, lunch said "rice 150 g" with the values of raw rice, but she weighed it cooked. And the oil in the pan was not logged at all. Those two things alone were hundreds of calories a day. Weighing pays off, as long as you know what you are weighing.

## Why weigh at all

Eyeballing fails even people who are honest about it. A classic NEJM study (Lichtman et al. 1992, PMID 1454084) measured ten people who said they ate under 1,200 calories and could not lose weight. Their energy expenditure was normal, within 5% of predicted values. The gap was in the log: they underreported intake by 47% on average and overreported activity by 51%. No bad intent needed, estimates simply drift.

A review of 22 studies on self-monitoring (Burke et al., J Am Diet Assoc 2011, PMID 21185970) found that tracking food is consistently linked to more weight loss. The authors add that study quality was limited. For practice that is enough for me: a food log is a cheap tool and it works better when the numbers in it are right.

## Raw or cooked: both work, never mix them

This is the most common mistake I see in food logs. Rice, pasta, legumes and grains soak up water when cooked, so the portion gets heavier but has the same calories as dry. Meat loses water, so cooked meat weighs less.

Rough numbers (exact values depend on the type and cooking time; yield tables are published for example by the US Department of Agriculture):

- **Rice:** 100 g raw becomes about 250 to 300 g cooked. Raw has around 350 kcal per 100 g, cooked around 130.
- **Pasta:** 100 g dry becomes about 200 to 250 g cooked. Dry around 360 kcal per 100 g, cooked around 150.
- **Chicken breast:** 100 g raw leaves about 70 to 75 g after roasting. The calories stay in the piece, they are just spread over less weight.
- **Potatoes boiled in their skin:** weight barely changes, hard to get wrong.

The trap: 150 g of cooked rice is about 195 calories. Log it as raw and your app adds over 500. The other way round, weigh it raw and pick "cooked" in the app and you erase almost two thirds of what you actually ate.

My rule for clients: **weigh it the way the entry is logged.** The simplest is to weigh raw and dry, because package labels are almost always for the dry product. Always check whether the entry in your app says raw or cooked.

## How to weigh when you cook for the whole family

1. Weigh the empty pot once and note it.
2. Cook, say 300 g of raw rice.
3. Weigh the pot with cooked rice and subtract the pot. Say you get 840 g.
4. Now you know 840 g cooked equals 300 g raw. If you serve yourself 210 g, you ate a quarter, so 75 g of raw rice.

Same for stews, risotto or soup. Count the whole pot once, then weigh only your portion.

## Where the unweighed calories hide

- **Oil and butter.** A tablespoon of oil is around 120 calories. Pouring by eye easily makes it two. Weigh it or measure it with a spoon and log it with every meal.
- **Tasting while cooking and finishing the kids' plates.** A few bites here and there can add up to an extra meal.
- **Spreads and dressings.** A "thin layer" of butter varies by tens of grams.
- **Drinks.** Juice, milk in coffee, the weekend beer.

## How long does it make sense to weigh food

You do not have to weigh for life. The scale is a tool to calibrate your eye. With clients I do it like this: two to four weeks of weighing everything. After that people can picture 150 g of meat or 200 g of potatoes, and it is enough to weigh calorie dense things like oils, nuts, cheese and sides. When the scale stalls, we go back to full weighing for a week and see where the eye drifted. How much to eat in the first place is covered in [calorie deficit: how much to eat](https://martinbarna.cz/en/blog/calorie-deficit-how-much-to-eat.html).

The goal is that you can do this on your own one day and do not need me for it.

## FAQ

### Should I weigh meat raw or cooked?
Raw, if that is how it is logged in your app. It is more precise because it does not change with how long it was in the oven. If you weigh it cooked, pick a cooked entry.

### What kitchen scale should I buy?
Any digital scale with a tare function and 1 g precision. Tare lets you zero the plate or pot and weigh only the food.

### Will weighing food make me obsessed?
With most clients I see the opposite: after a few weeks they estimate much better and stop being afraid of food. If weighing gives you anxiety or a feeling of losing control around food, stop and talk to a professional.
