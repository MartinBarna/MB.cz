"I could do this if I lived alone." I hear this from clients a lot. Your partner wants pizza, the kids have cookies in the drawer and Sunday lunch at grandma's comes with cake. You are losing weight and everybody else is not. The good news: you can make it work without two kitchens and without fights at the table.

## Support at home shows up in the data

A 1999 American study (Wing and Jeffery, J Consult Clin Psychol, PMID 10028217) recruited 166 people either alone or with three friends or family members. Half of them also got social support training. The difference was big. Of those recruited alone without support, 76% finished the program and 24% fully kept their weight loss from month 4 to month 10. Of those recruited with friends and given support, 95% finished and 66% kept it.

You do not need a weight loss buddy. It is worth talking to the people at home and getting at least one ally.

## Your fat loss spreads at home

This is my favourite part. The large Look AHEAD trial also followed the spouses of participants, who got no treatment themselves (Gorin et al., Int J Obes 2008, PMID 18762804). Spouses of people in the intensive program lost 2.2 kg in a year on average, spouses in the control group 0.2 kg. They ate less and there was less high fat food at home.

A newer trial (Gorin et al., Obesity 2018, PMID 29388385) found a similar effect in 130 couples. Untreated spouses lost about 2 kg in six months and almost a third of them at least 3% of their weight. The partners' weights moved together.

The changes you make for yourself often spill over to the others. No persuading needed.

## One meal, different portions

Cooking two dinners ends within a month. Cook one meal that everyone assembles their own way, the same idea I teach in [flexible dieting](https://martinbarna.cz/en/blog/flexible-dieting.html).

- **Protein, side and vegetables separately.** Roast chicken, potatoes, vegetables. You take more meat and vegetables and fewer potatoes, your partner serves themselves as usual.
- **Sauce and fat added on the plate.** Cream, butter or cheese, everyone adds their own. You take a spoon, the others as much as they like.
- **Family classics, lighter.** Oven baked chicken schnitzel, a stew with more meat and less fat. Most families enjoy it just as much and you do not cook separately.
- **Plan for the weekend lunch at grandma's.** Lighter breakfast, a smaller side and one piece of cake you enjoy without guilt. One lunch will not hurt you, a weekend pattern will, as I explain [here](https://martinbarna.cz/en/blog/weekend-overeating.html).

## Sweets at home

The kids can have sweets and you do not have to take them away because of your diet. The point is to keep them out of your sight.

- Sweets in a closed cupboard, ideally in an opaque box.
- Fruit and small packs of nuts on the counter, yogurts at eye level in the fridge.
- Buy sweets that do not tempt you. Everyone has their weakness and it does not need to live in the house.

Diet rules for adults do not belong on children. Their weight and food are a topic for the pediatrician.

## The talk with your partner

One open conversation helps more than a hundred hints. A few lines that work:

- "I don't want you to eat differently because of me. Just don't offer when I say no."
- "It helps me if we keep sweets in the cupboard."
- "Instead of the cake shop on Sunday, can we go for a walk and then coffee?"

When your partner says "just one bite, it won't hurt", they rarely mean harm. They do not know what it means for you. Say it specifically and repeat it three times if needed.

And if there is no support at home and there will not be, look for it elsewhere: a friend who is also losing weight, a colleague at lunch, a community with the same goal. In coaching we handle this conversation with clients all the time and set up food that works for the whole household.

## FAQ

### Do I have to cook diet food for my family?
No. Cook normal meals, just split them into components so everyone serves themselves. You take more protein and vegetables, the others eat as usual.

### What if my partner sabotages me?
Often it is not bad intent, more a fear of change or a feeling that they will have to eat "diet food" too. Tell them specifically what helps and what does not. If it keeps happening, it is worth looking at what is behind it, possibly with a relationship professional.

### Should I involve my kids?
Not in your weight loss, but in a healthier lifestyle, gladly. Cooking together, more time outside, fruit on the table. Leave their weight and diet to the pediatrician.
