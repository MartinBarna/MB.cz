How to lose belly fat: what works (and what is a myth)

📅 13 October 2026 · ⏱️ 7 min read

Author: Martin Barna · Published 13 October 2026

You want to know how to lose belly fat, and preferably in 14 days. The unpleasant part first: spot reduction does not exist. No exercise and no miracle food burns fat from your belly specifically. The belly gets smaller when you lose fat from the whole body. The way there is simple and proven. It is just not fast.

Short answer: Spot reduction does not exist, so sit-ups will not burn belly fat. The belly shrinks when you lose fat from the whole body with a mild, sustainable calorie deficit. Lift weights 2 to 4 times a week and eat about 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg, so you keep muscle and lose fat.

Why you cannot lose belly fat locally

One of the most common fitness myths says: do sit-ups and your belly fat disappears. It does not. Your body does not burn fat from the spot you are working. It pulls from fat stores all over the body, based on its own genetics and hormones. You can't control where fat is stored first and where it leaves last. For many people the belly is unfortunately the last place to get lean.

A controlled trial tested this directly. 24 healthy, sedentary adults were split into two groups. One group did seven ab exercises five days a week for six weeks, the other did no exercise. Both kept the same calorie intake. Abdominal fat and waist circumference did not change in a significant way, although the exercise group could do more curl-ups at the end, a sign of better muscular endurance (Vispute, 2011, PMID 21804427).

You have abs under there. Everyone does. You just can't see them through the fat layer. Ab exercises make the muscles stronger and firmer, but on their own they won't melt the fat on top of them.

You don't need to grind out hundreds of sit-ups. You focus on what drives fat loss across the whole body. The rest of this article is about that.

What works: a calorie deficit is the base

For fat to go (and your belly with it), you need to take in less energy than your body uses over a long period. That is called a calorie deficit, and every fat-loss method works through it. No diet, food or exercise gets around it. When someone loses weight 'without counting calories', they got into a deficit some way: smaller portions, less sugar, more movement.

A mild deficit works best: one you can keep for months without losing muscle for nothing. How to set it, and why crash diets lead to yo-yo dieting, is in calorie deficit: how much to eat. To get a first estimate of how many kcal (calories) you should eat, use the calculator. It takes a few seconds.

You don't need to ban any food. I teach flexible dieting. You track macros and total intake, not a list of 'forbidden' foods. Beer, chocolate and pizza fit into a deficit if you watch the total balance. In my practice, this is a big reason clients stick with it for the long run.

Strength training and protein: keep your muscle while you lose fat

In a deficit the body loses fat, but you can lose muscle with it. You don't want that. Muscle gives your body its shape, and thanks to it your belly looks firm after the weight loss instead of deflated. Two things prevent the loss: strength training and enough protein.

  • Strength training 2 to 4 times a week tells your body to keep its muscle even while you lose weight. It doesn't have to be a gym. Home training with your body weight or dumbbells is enough.
  • Enough protein (roughly 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg of body weight) protects muscle mass and fills you up more, so a deficit is easier to hold. More in how much protein.
  • Walking and steps count for a lot. They raise your total energy use without wearing you out. How many steps make sense is in how many steps a day.

Women often worry that weights will make them 'bulky'. Most women find this does not happen. With much lower testosterone than men, muscle grows slowly, and getting bigger takes years of dedicated training and eating in a surplus. Strength training is what gives a body a firm, shaped look.

Sleep and stress (and the 'hormonal belly')

You can have a perfect meal plan and perfect training and still get stuck if you sleep badly and live in constant stress. Too little sleep raises appetite, makes decisions at the fridge worse and makes it harder to hold a deficit. I covered the effect of sleep in sleep and fat loss. It is often a bigger factor than you would think.

People talk a lot about the hormonal belly: cortisol, the stress hormone, is blamed for belly fat. To be precise: chronic stress and poor sleep can slow fat loss, mostly because they make the deficit harder to hold. The energy balance still applies. But hormones do not build a belly on their own against a deficit. Managing stress and sleep makes it easier to keep your diet and training in place. If stress feels like more than you can handle, talk to your doctor or a mental health professional. In the Czech Republic, the free crisis line is 116 123.

Important: if you suspect a hormonal problem (thyroid, PCOS, insulin resistance) or you take medication, talk to your doctor. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, or you have a history of an eating disorder, talk to your doctor before you start a calorie deficit. I'm a coach, not a doctor. I don't diagnose and I don't replace medical care. The practical steps for sleep and stress are open to everyone though: a regular routine, fewer screens in the evening, movement, walks.

How to lose belly fat in 14 days? Realistic expectations

Honestly, a flat belly in 14 days is not realistic if you start with a larger amount of fat. A reasonable, sustainable pace is roughly 0.3 to 0.7 kg of fat per week. In two weeks that is 2 x 0.3 to 0.7 kg = 0.6 to 1.4 kg of fat. You will notice it on the scale, but it is not a finished belly. Whoever promises a 'flat belly in 14 days' is either selling a miracle or counting on water loss that comes back after a few days.

What you can do in 14 days is start habits that work: set a deficit, start strength training, raise protein, fix your sleep. That is the part you control, and in a few months it brings a result that lasts.

One more thing people forget. Fat around the organs (visceral fat) is linked to a higher risk of metabolic problems. Losing fat overall usually lowers it too, a benefit the mirror does not show. That is one more reason to skip miracles and use the proven method.

Summary: the plan that works

The plan in five lines:

  • Accept that spot reduction does not exist. The belly shrinks with overall fat loss.
  • Set a mild, sustainable calorie deficit (flexible is fine, no forbidden foods).
  • Lift 2 to 4 times a week and eat enough protein, so you lose fat and keep muscle.
  • Watch your sleep and stress. They matter more than they look.
  • Keep realistic expectations: plan for a few months of habits that last.

Frequently asked questions

How do I lose belly fat in 14 days?

You won't get a flat belly in 14 days if you start with a larger amount of fat. A realistic pace is roughly 0.3 to 0.7 kg of fat per week. What you can do in two weeks is start habits that work: a mild calorie deficit, strength training, enough protein and better sleep. Those bring a result that lasts.

Do ab exercises help lose belly fat?

Not by themselves. Spot reduction does not exist. Ab exercises make the muscles stronger and firmer, but they don't burn the fat right above them. The belly shows up only when you lose fat from the whole body through a calorie deficit. Training your abs is a nice add-on to the calorie deficit.

Is there a hormonal belly?

Chronic stress and poor sleep can slow fat loss, mostly because they make the deficit harder to hold. The energy balance still applies: hormones do not build a belly against a calorie deficit. If you suspect a hormonal disorder or you take medication, talk to your doctor and don't try to solve it on your own.
