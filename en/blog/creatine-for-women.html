Creatine for women: not just for muscle

📅 22 October 2026 · ⏱️ 5 min read

Author: Martin Barna · Published 22 October 2026

Creatine still carries a label in a lot of heads: "a supplement for jacked guys." That is a waste. It is the best-studied supplement we have, and one of the safest, and women may get even more from it than men do. Reviews from 2025 and 2026 show that creatine reaches well past muscle: to strength, body composition, mood, and healthy aging.

Short answer: Does creatine make sense for women?
Yes, especially if you lift. A 2025 review finds that creatine supports strength, performance, and body composition in women, mostly together with strength training. Take 3 to 5 g of creatine monohydrate every day, including rest days, on top of the basics (protein, lifting, sleep, consistency). In pregnancy, while breastfeeding, or with kidney disease, talk to your doctor before you start.

Why women in particular

Women naturally store less creatine than men, and they often get less of it from food (mostly from meat). That is exactly why they can respond well to topping it up. A large 2025 review of creatine in women's health sums it up like this: creatine supports strength, performance, and body composition in women, especially combined with strength training, and it also looks promising for mood and cognitive function, including a possible easing of depressive symptoms.

Not just muscle: the brain, and aging

A 2026 systematic review (more than 1,500 older adults) found that five of six studies linked creatine with better memory and attention in people over 55. And a randomized trial in older adults showed that creatine added on top of strength training in inflammatory and performance markers. For women in and after menopause, it is worth knowing that higher creatine doses are also tied to support for bone, muscle, and mental well-being, which is exactly where things get harder with age.

After menopause: 5 g a day, plus lifting

With clients after menopause I keep hearing the same thing: creatine is something "gym women" take. A meta-analysis by Naddafha and colleagues (2026, PMID 42141930) pools 7 randomized trials in 608 postmenopausal women. The average gain: +0.37 kg of muscle (about 0.8 lb) and +7.5 kg on the leg press (about 16.5 lb). That is a solid number once you know how fast muscle disappears after menopause. It held at a dose of 5 g a day combined with strength training. Doses up to 3 g, without lifting, did not work in this review. The powder on its own, with no weights, was not enough in that data.

A wider view comes from Chilibeck and colleagues (2017, PMID 29138605): 22 randomized trials, 721 people aged 57 to 70. With strength training, creatine added +1.37 kg of muscle on average (about 3 lb). Those trials did not show liver or kidney damage. In coaching I treat it practically. If you lift after menopause, 5 g of monohydrate a day is the dose at which these reviews show a gain in muscle and strength. It is cheap, safe according to that data, and it fits the basics (protein, sleep, consistency). Without the weights, it barely registered in these groups.

And what about the myths?

  • "It will make me bulky." No. Creatine will not turn you into a bodybuilder. It helps you get stronger and keep muscle, which is a win for a woman's physique, not a threat.
  • "It holds water." A little extra water sits inside the muscle, not as "bloat." For most women it is negligible, and it has nothing to do with a bloated belly.
  • "It is a steroid / it is unhealthy." It is not. It is a compound your body makes on its own and also gets from food. It is among the best-tested supplements we have for safety.

How to take it

  • Creatine monohydrate. The cheapest form, and the one with the most research. You do not need anything more expensive.
  • 3 to 5 g a day, every day (including days you do not train). A loading phase is not required. It just takes a few extra weeks for stores to fill.
  • Any time of day, fine with a meal. Do not overthink the timing.
  • It works best as a pair with strength training. The powder alone does not do miracles.

What to take from this

Creatine is not a men's product. For a woman who lifts, it is a cheap, safe extra insurance policy: for strength, physique, and possibly a sharper head and a better mood. The order still holds. Basics first (enough protein, strength training, sleep, consistency), supplements after. Creatine complements that base. It does not replace it.
