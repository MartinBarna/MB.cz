Strength training improves mobility as well as stretching

📅 22 October 2026 · ⏱️ 8 min read

Author: Martin Barna · Published 22 October 2026

The unwritten rule is still around: if you want to be more flexible and more mobile, stretch. Lifting, people say, "shortens" muscle and turns you into a stiff brick. Look at what we know now, and it comes out the other way. Strength training taken through a full range of motion improves mobility about as well as stretching does. Unlike stretching, it also gives you strength and muscle. Here is what the studies show, why it works, and how to use it.

Short answer: Yes, if you lift through a full range of motion. A meta-analysis of 55 studies in Sports Medicine (2023) found that strength training increases range of motion about as much as stretching does. Unlike stretching, it also adds strength and muscle.

Two words first: mobility and flexibility

Here is the difference. Flexibility is the muscle's ability to be lengthened: how far you can get into an end position. Mobility is a step further: it is range of motion at a joint that you can also control and hold on purpose. In plain terms, it is not only getting into a deep squat. It is being able to work there. That is where strength training has the edge. It does not only hand you bare range. It hands you range where you also have strength and control.

What the studies show: strength raises range about like stretching

This is not a gym opinion. It is a fairly clear picture from the research. A large systematic review and meta-analysis from 2023 (Sports Medicine) pooled 55 studies and landed on a simple result: strength training increases range of motion by a moderate amount. That increase was not statistically different from what stretching alone can do, or from combining strength work and stretching. In other words, for range of motion, lifting works about as well as stretching [1].

A newer meta-analysis from 2025 (Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research) confirmed it across 36 studies and nearly 1,500 people: strength training clearly improves flexibility (a moderate effect). One detail is worth keeping: more intense training had a bigger effect on mobility. The authors singled out the eccentric phase, the lowering, braking part of the rep. It can lengthen muscle fascicles (the bundles inside a muscle) and increase range at the joint, and at the same time it adds strength [2].

Third, a head-to-head comparison. A randomized trial from 2024 (BMC Sports Science, Medicine and Rehabilitation) put people into three groups: strength training through a full range, static stretching, and a control group. After eight weeks, both training groups improved flexibility by about the same amount (measured with a standard forward-bend test). The difference was what else they gained: the group that lifted also added strength on top of the mobility. Stretching on its own, as you would expect, does not add strength [3].

Full-range strength training improves mobility about as well as stretching does. It also adds strength and muscle. You get both from the same work.

Why it works at all

There is no trick to it. When you load muscles through a full range of motion, you go to a deep squat, you lower the bar to where you feel a real stretch, and only then do you lift. You train the muscle in its lengthened position. Load at a long muscle length is what matters for mobility:

  • The muscle learns to produce force at end range too. That is exactly what a muscle you have only stretched is missing. You can get there, but you cannot control it there.
  • The eccentric (lowering) phase under load lengthens muscle fascicles. The body adapts to being strong while it is stretched, and the range actually increases.
  • Joints, tendons, and the nervous system get used to full range as normal. They stop treating it as dangerous ground they have to hold back.

The other side of that: if everything in the gym is a half rep (partial squats, presses you never finish, curls through a tiny arc), you never really load the muscles in a stretch. Then you can feel "stiff" even though you train. It is not the lifting. It is the cut-down range.

The point of the whole article: full range of motion

Take this with you. Not every kind of lifting improves mobility. The range you train through decides it. Half reps will not give you mobility. A full, controlled range will. If you want mobility from your strength work, train like this:

  • Squat to depth (as far as your hips and your technique allow), instead of stopping above parallel.
  • Romanian deadlifts and deadlifts with a real hamstring stretch at the bottom.
  • Lunges and Bulgarian split squats to full depth, which are useful for hip mobility.
  • Presses and push-ups through a full range: chest all the way down, not a short tap and back up.
  • Pull-ups and rows from a full hang: reach all the way at the bottom and open the shoulders.

And on all of it, use a controlled eccentric: do not drop the weight. Brake it on purpose for 2-3 seconds. Mobility is built most in that lowering phase, while the muscle is long.

Does that mean stretching is pointless?

No. Stretching has a place. If your range is genuinely limited somewhere, stretching after training or at another time can help open it, and a lot of people simply feel better when they do it. The point of this article is not "throw stretching out." The useful part is simpler: you do not have to add hours of stretching to be mobile. If you lift through a full range, you build mobility in the same session, along with strength and muscle. And if you have no time or no appetite for stretching, that is not a loss.

What to do with this

  • Train through a full range of motion. Squats to depth, pulls with a stretch, presses all the way down. Range is what turns lifting into a mobility tool as well.
  • Brake the eccentric. 2-3 controlled seconds on the way down. That is the most valuable part of the rep for mobility.
  • Do not be afraid of a lengthened position under load, as long as the technique is sound and the weight is reasonable. It is safe, and it is exactly where mobility is built.
  • Add range gradually. You will go deeper over time, not by forcing it in the first session. Technique and progression still run the show.
  • Keep stretching as an extra, not a duty. If it feels good, do it. It is not what guarantees your mobility. Full range in the gym does.

So the next time someone tells you that "weights only make you stiff," and that flexibility comes only from stretching, you already know how that holds up. Full-range strength training gives you mobility comparable to stretching. It also gives you strength and muscle, which stretching never adds. Train the whole range, brake the eccentric, and let the muscles work while they are long. Be Effective!

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between mobility and flexibility?

Flexibility is the muscle's ability to be lengthened: how far you can get into an end position. Mobility is range of motion at a joint that you can also control and hold on purpose.

Why does full-range lifting improve mobility?

You train the muscle in its lengthened position, so it learns to produce force at end range too. The eccentric (lowering) phase under load lengthens muscle fascicles, and the joints, tendons, and nervous system get used to full range as normal.

Which exercises should I use if I want lifting to improve mobility?

A squat to depth (as far as your hips and your technique allow), Romanian deadlifts and deadlifts with a real hamstring stretch, lunges and Bulgarian split squats to full depth, presses and push-ups through a full range, and pull-ups and rows from a full hang. On all of them, brake the weight on purpose for 2-3 seconds.

Will lifting make me stiff?

You can feel stiff if everything in the gym is a half rep, such as partial squats, because you never really load the muscles in a stretch. The cut-down range is what does it.

So is stretching useless?

No. If your range is limited somewhere, stretching can help open it, and if your mobility is limited or you have a health problem, talk to a doctor or a physical therapist. You just do not have to add hours of stretching to be mobile if you lift through a full range.
