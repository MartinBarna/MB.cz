Spot reduction is a myth: crunches do not flatten the belly

📅 13 October 2026 · ⏱️ 7 min read

Author: Martin Barna · Published 13 October 2026

"I do crunches every day and my belly is still there." I have heard that from a hundred people. The logic sounds reasonable: I want a flat stomach, so I work the stomach. The body does not work that way. Fat does not vanish from the place you train. You can do a thousand crunches and the fat at the waist will not notice.

Short answer: No. You will not lose belly fat by doing crunches every day. Fat does not leave the place you train. It is released from the whole body, and the order is run mostly by genetics and hormones. What moves belly fat is a mild calorie deficit, about 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg, full-body strength training, and 8 to 10 thousand steps a day.

What "spot reduction" is supposed to mean

Spot reduction is the idea that if you load a muscle in one place, the body starts burning the fat directly on top of it. Train the abs, belly fat goes. Circle the hips, the hips shrink. The arms lose the soft bit because you did kickbacks. It sounds like common sense: the muscle is working, so it takes energy from next door.

The muscle does not take its energy from the neighboring fat. Fat tissue is not released locally according to what you are loading. It is released from the whole body, it travels in the blood to where it is needed, and the order in which places shrink is run mostly by genetics and hormones. Your exercise plan does not rewrite that. The practical version for the belly is in how to lose belly fat. The same rule for the face is in how to lose face fat.

What the body does instead

In a calorie deficit the body has to cover the gap between what you eat and what you burn. It draws on stores, across the whole body at once, not from one particular pad. Fat is broken into fatty acids, they travel in the blood, and they are burned where the muscles need them. An ab muscle during a crunch can perfectly well burn fat that was released from the hip or the thigh.

The order is the body's own. For some people the face and the chest go first, and the belly and hips go last. That is not a bad training plan. It is your genetics. Often "the last to leave is the place that bothers you most": classically the belly and the hips.

Fat does not leave the place you train. It leaves from the whole body, in an order genetics set, not the number of crunches.

What the studies show

This is one of the better tested ideas in the gym. In controlled studies people strengthened only one leg or only one arm for weeks, and researchers compared fat loss on the trained limb with the other one. If spot reduction worked, the trained limb would have lost clearly more fat. It did not. Any gap between the limbs was so small it has no practical meaning, and fat fell across the whole body.

Studies with weeks of high-volume ab work land the same way. The ab muscle got stronger and endurance went up. Waist circumference and belly fat barely changed versus a control group. The conclusion is the same every time: training a muscle strengthens that muscle. By itself it is not enough to remove the fat on top of it.

Why you still cannot see the muscle

This is the part that confuses people most. You have abs. Everyone does. It is a muscle like any other, and it grows when you load it. The reason you cannot see it is not a weak muscle. It is a layer of fat lying over it.

Picture the ab muscle as a shape, and the fat as a blanket on top. You can build the shape under the blanket with a thousand crunches. You still will not see it until the blanket comes off. The blanket comes off only with a drop in body fat for the whole body, a deficit. How to set one is in calorie deficit: how much to eat.

What to do if you want the abs to show

You do not need a miracle ab exercise. You need lower body fat, and your own anatomy does the rest. The levers are the same for everyone:

  • A mild calorie deficit. This is the engine. Without a deficit the fat does not go, no matter what you train.
  • Enough protein. About 1.6 to 2.2 g per kg. It keeps muscle while you go down, so there is a shape under the fat. See how much protein.
  • Full-body strength training. It builds muscle, raises expenditure, and shapes how you will look once the fat is lower. Keeping muscle in a deficit is in build muscle in a calorie deficit.
  • Steps and ordinary movement. Cheap expenditure, and most people skip it. 8 to 10 thousand steps a day does more than an hour of crunches. See how many steps a day.

I see this with clients all the time. People who chased ab exercises for years "find" the abs within a few months once they sort the food and drop body fat, not once they add another set of crunches.

The grain of truth

Training the abs is worth doing. Not as a spot-reduction tool. A strong midsection holds posture, protects the back in a squat and a deadlift, and stabilizes you in almost everything you do. That is a real reason to train the abs and the deeper muscles.

A better developed ab muscle will also show more once the fat is lower, so the training pays you back. Put it in as one piece of the plan, not as a spell for a flat stomach. Next time someone sells you "an exercise to burn belly fat", you know: you cannot burn a local pad by grinding the muscle under it. The result comes when you sort the whole.

The myth and the reality, side by side:

  • "I train abs, so my belly shrinks." Fat is released from the whole body by genetics, not from the spot under the muscle.
  • "A thousand crunches equals a flat stomach." Crunches strengthen the muscle. The fat on top comes off only with a deficit for the whole body.
  • "I do not have abs." You do. They are hidden under a layer of fat that has to come off.
  • "Training abs is pointless." It is useful for a strong midsection and for posture. Not for local fat.
