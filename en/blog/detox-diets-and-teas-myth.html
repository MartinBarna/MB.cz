Detox diets and teas are a myth

📅 23 October 2026 · ⏱️ 4 min read

Author: Martin Barna · Published 23 October 2026

After the holidays or a week of partying comes that feeling: "I need to cleanse." And the internet is right there: a three-day juice cleanse, a month of detox tea, foot pads that turn brown overnight from "toxins pulled out." It sounds logical and looks like self-care. But the whole category rests on one blank that never gets filled in: which toxin exactly is being removed from you? Marketing will not name it. And that is the first clue someone is messing with you.

Short answer: Do detox diets and detox teas work? No. Your body has detox built in: the liver and kidneys work twenty-four hours a day, and no juice, tea, or powder makes them run faster. The kilos (lbs) you drop on a juice cleanse are mostly water and stored sugar (glycogen), and within a few days of normal eating they come back. The fat stayed where it was.

Your body has detox built in, and it runs nonstop

Here is the thing no tea seller will tell you: you already have a treatment plant. It is called the liver and the kidneys, and they work on it twenty-four hours a day, whether you sleep, eat, or do nothing. The liver breaks down alcohol, medications, and metabolic byproducts and packages them so the body can get rid of them. The kidneys then filter them from the blood and send them out in urine. Add the lungs, skin, and gut, and you have a complete system that runs on its own.

No juice, no tea, and no powder can make that liver or those kidneys "run faster" or "clean deeper." Either they work normally (and then they do not need help), or they do not, and that is a serious diagnosis you do not fix with ginger lemonade, but with a nephrologist. There is nothing in between.

The test every detox product fails

Try this yourself next time. When you hit anything with a "detox" label, ask one question: which specific toxin does it remove, and how does it get it out of the body? Watch how fast the answer dissolves into fog.

You will hear "toxins," "impurities," "deposits," "residual substances," "acids." Plenty of words, zero chemical names. Because if the company named a real substance, you could measure it in blood before and after, and it would show that nothing happened. The fog is there on purpose. It is a feature, not an accident.

No detox product will ever name the toxin it removes. If it did, you could measure it. And you would measure nothing.

Why you "lose weight" on a detox, and why it comes back

People write me excited: "three days on juices and I am down two kilos (about 4 lbs)!" I believe the numbers. The explanation just does not hold. When you drink only juices or tea for three days, you eat a fraction of your usual calories. The body dips into glycogen (stored sugar in muscle and liver), and every gram of glycogen holds roughly three grams of water. As glycogen goes, the water goes with it.

What shows on the scale is mostly emptied water and stored sugar, not burned fat. As soon as you eat normally again, glycogen and water return within a few days, and so do those "lost" kilos. I see it with clients all the time: they show up after some cleanse, the scale jumped down, and a week later they are right back where they started. No fat disappeared. Water just moved around.

Some "detox" products are just a laxative

For part of the category it is even simpler, and uglier. Read the ingredients on detox teas "for gut cleansing" or "to flatten the belly" and you often find herbs with a laxative effect (senna, buckthorn) or ingredients that dehydrate you. The result? You empty out and dry out. That is not detox. That is diarrhea in a pouch.

And it is not harmless. Stimulant laxatives used regularly mess up electrolytes, can cause cramps and dehydration, and with long-term use can make the gut depend on them. You pay to feel worse and to get the sense that it "works," because something left your body.

What the liver and kidneys actually appreciate (and it is free)

This part always disappoints people a little, because it is not for sale. Want to actually help your organs? You do not need to buy anything. Just do things you already know, only put them together:

  • Enough water. Kidneys filter better when you are not dried out. No miracle water, plain tap water.
  • Protein and fiber. Steady energy, satiety, and a healthier gut and microbiome.
  • Vegetables every day. Fiber, potassium, antioxidants: real support, not marketing.
  • Less alcohol. This eases the liver more than any tea you buy for that purpose.
  • Sleep and movement. Recovery and blood flow do more for "cleansing" than a three-day starvation diet.

None of that is sexy, and nobody builds a store around it. It does work, and you do it for life, not for three days.

Where there is a grain of truth

To be fair: the post-holiday urge is not silly. When after a week of sweets, heavy meals, and wine you reach for a bigger plate of vegetables, cut back on alcohol, drink more water, and move more, you do yourself good and you will feel better. That effect is real and it makes sense.

But that is not the credit of a "detox product." That is normal good eating that you set up on purpose for a few days. No tea or juice plays a role in it. What matters is that you ate sensibly for a few days. You can call it a detox if that makes you happy. Just do not pay for it, and do not expect it to "clean" you better than your own liver, which was doing it the whole time for free.

So the next time a pricey cleanse tempts you, translate it in your head to "I will eat better and drink less for a few days." That is the whole secret. And you can do that yourself.

Frequently asked questions

Why do I lose two kilos (about 4 lbs) in three days on a juice cleanse?

You eat a fraction of your usual calories and your body dips into glycogen, the stored sugar in muscle and liver. Every gram of glycogen holds roughly three grams of water, so the water leaves with it. Once you eat normally again, glycogen and water come back.

How do I know a detox product does not work?

Ask which specific toxin it removes and how it gets it out of the body. You will only get words like toxins or deposits with no chemical name. If the company named a real substance, you could measure it in blood before and after.

Are detox teas for cleaning the gut safe?

They often contain herbs with a laxative effect (senna, buckthorn) or ingredients that dehydrate you. Stimulant laxatives used regularly mess up electrolytes, can cause cramps and dehydration, and with long-term use can make the gut depend on them.

What actually helps the liver and kidneys?

Enough water, protein and fiber, vegetables every day, less alcohol, sleep, and movement. None of that needs to be bought as a detox product.
