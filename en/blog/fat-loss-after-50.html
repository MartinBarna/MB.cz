Weight loss after 50: metabolism, protein and strength training

📅 13 October 2026 · ⏱️ 6 min read

Author: Martin Barna · Published 13 October 2026

At 40, skipping bread was enough and two kilos were gone within a month. After 50 the same move does almost nothing, and it is easy to conclude that a slow metabolism is to blame. I see this in clients regularly. The data show a different picture, and above all they point to a different plan.

Short answer: Metabolism after 50 does not drop the way people say. Muscle and daily movement drop. Keep a mild deficit of 15 to 20% below your energy use, eat 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg, and lift weights two to three times a week. If you take medication for thyroid, diabetes or blood pressure, talk to your doctor about the exact setup.

Metabolism after 50 does not drop as much as people say

The biggest analysis of daily energy expenditure so far (Pontzer et al., Science 2021, PMID 34385400) measured people from eight days to 95 years old with doubly labeled water. Adjusted for fat-free mass, meaning everything except fat, daily energy use stays stable between ages 20 and 60. It starts to fall only in older people. At 50 you are still inside that stable range.

What changes is what that fat-free mass is made of, and how much you walk in a day. Muscle goes down and movement goes down, because work is more sedentary and in the evening you don't feel like going anywhere. Both pull energy use down, but they have less to do with age itself than people assume. I wrote more in the slow metabolism article.

A deficit you can keep for a whole year

A kilo of fat holds roughly 7,700 kcal (calories). That is why a mild, long deficit works better than starving for most people: 15 to 20% below your daily energy use. If your maintenance is 2,200 kcal, the target is around 1,800 kcal. Faster is possible too, but you pay with more muscle loss and worse sleep, and after 50 those are the last things you want to give up.

The second half of the work is accuracy of logging. People underestimate their intake by 20 to 50% as a normal thing, and the oil in the pan, nuts and a sweetened coffee hide easily in that. Before you cut a second time, give it a week on the scale and in a notebook. The calculation is in calorie deficit: how much to eat, and you can get a starting estimate in the calculator.

If you take medication for thyroid, diabetes or blood pressure, or you are dealing with a new diagnosis, treat the numbers in this article as a guide and talk to your doctor about the exact setup.

Protein keeps muscle when you cut calories

A meta-analysis of twenty randomized trials in people over 50 (Kim et al., Nutrition Reviews 2016, PMID 26883880) compared weight loss with lower and higher protein intake. The cut-off was 25% of energy, or 1.0 g per kg per day. The conclusion of the abstract is short: older adults on the higher protein intake kept more muscle mass and lost more fat.

In practice I aim for 1.6 to 2.2 g of protein per kg of body weight and spread it over all meals of the day. One big evening portion usually won't get you there, because that much meat or quark doesn't fit in. For a feel of how much that is in normal food:

  • 150 g chicken breast: about 33 g protein
  • 250 g semi-skimmed quark (a fresh cheese, similar to strained Greek yogurt): about 30 g
  • 3 eggs: about 19 g
  • 30 g whey protein: about 24 g

A person weighing 75 kg gets to 120 to 140 g a day with three meals and one snack. For more on sources and myths, see how much protein, and use the protein tool to compare foods by protein per 100 kcal.

Strength training decides whether you lose weight or get weaker

This is the part people over 50 skip most often, and it has the hardest data. A study of 160 older adults with obesity (Villareal et al., New England Journal of Medicine 2017, PMID 28514618) split participants into four groups: weight loss with aerobic training, with strength training, with both combined, and a control group with no program.

Body weight dropped by 9% in all exercise groups, so the scale showed no difference between them. The difference was in what disappeared from the body. Fat-free mass dropped 5% with aerobic training alone, but only 2% with strength training and 3% with the combination. The combination also did best on the physical performance test, with the score up 21% against 14% for each of the two alone. The authors sum it up like this: of the compared options, combined strength and aerobic training was the most effective.

In practice that means strength training for the whole body two to three times a week, six to eight exercises, and adding weight or reps step by step. Treat walking as the base of the day that everything stands on. I cover how many steps a day separately.

Women in menopause: why the scale lies

The long-term SWAN study (Greendale et al., JCI Insight 2019, PMID 30843880) tracked body composition around the last period. With the start of menopause the rate of fat gain doubled and fat-free mass began to fall. Both continued until about two years after the last period and then stopped. One interesting detail: body weight itself had been rising linearly before menopause and did not speed up at its start.

The practical point: the scale can show almost the same number while the ratio of fat to muscle changes. So after 50 don't track one number only. Add waist circumference, photos once a month and how much you can lift. Talk to your doctor about hormone therapy and menopause symptoms. You can't fix those with a meal plan.

Frequently asked questions

Is weight loss slower after 50 than at 30?

Usually a bit slower, mainly because of less muscle and less movement during the day. A pace of 0.5 to 0.7% of body weight per week is still reachable after 50, and you keep more muscle on it than on a faster pace.

Do I have to go to a gym?

You need a strength stimulus, but a gym is only one way to get it. At home you can do bodyweight squats, lunges, push-ups against the kitchen counter, and bands or two dumbbells. What matters is that the load grows step by step. Where you train is secondary.

How many kilos a month is reasonable after 50?

For most people 2 to 3 kg a month at the start, then less as body weight goes down. If the scale drops faster for several weeks in a row and your strength in training is falling too, you are cutting too much.
