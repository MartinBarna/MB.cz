Fat burners: what actually works, and what is wasted money

📅 22 October 2026 · ⏱️ 8 min read

Author: Martin Barna · Published 22 October 2026

I remember a client who showed up with a box of supplements that had cost him real money. "I take a fat burner, L-carnitine, and that green tea thing for metabolism, and it still will not move." Of course it will not move. Because the only thing that actually burns fat is not something you buy in a shop. Let us walk the "fat burner" shelf: what has at least some support in the science, and what is a pure marketing story.

Short answer: Do fat burners work for fat loss?
There is no magic fat burner. You burn fat only in a calorie deficit, and no supplement gets around that law. Caffeine has a real effect, and it is small, mostly as a performance aid before training. L-carnitine, CLA, raspberry ketones, garcinia, and detox teas are wasted money.

First, how this actually works in the body

The word "burner" makes people picture a pill that sets fat on fire the way lighter fluid sets up a grill. The body does not work like that. You release fat from a cell and burn it only when, over time, you take in less energy than you use. That is a calorie deficit, and it is the only lever that actually removes fat. No supplement gets around that law.

Most "fat burners" play a different game. They try to raise your energy expenditure by a few percent (thermogenesis), or to nudge fat out of the cell a little. Even if that worked exactly as the ad says, we are talking about tens of calories a day. Eat one extra bread roll and you are back at zero. A stimulant, or a capsule of extract, never beats what is on your plate.

The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) says it straight in its position stands on supplements: only a few compounds have reliable support, and none of them is a magic fat burner. Creatine, protein, caffeine for performance. The data is there. For "fat burners" the picture is much thinner, and for most ingredients it is basically zero.

Caffeine: this is where the science has something to say

Caffeine is the only one in the group you can talk about as a real effect, and even then it is smaller than people think. It does two things reasonably well. First, it raises energy expenditure a little, because it is a stimulant and it turns thermogenesis up. Second, and this is the main one, it is ergogenic: it improves performance, delays fatigue, and the session feels easier. You train more, and harder, and that is how you burn more, indirectly.

The catch: the effect on expenditure itself is small, and the body gets used to caffeine. What raised your heart rate and your will to work at the start is almost unnoticeable after a few weeks of taking it daily. You build a tolerance. So caffeine is not a "fat burner." It is a performance aid, and it makes sense to use it on purpose before training, not to pour it in from the morning as part of some miracle protocol.

Caffeine does not burn your fat. It helps you train the session that helps burn the fat. That is the whole difference, and it is worth understanding.

Green tea and EGCG: marginal, but at least real

The second most common player is green tea extract, specifically the catechin EGCG, often combined with caffeine. The theory makes sense: catechins may slow the breakdown of noradrenaline a little, and that can slightly support expenditure and fat oxidation.

What the data shows: the effect is marginal. We are talking about very small differences on the scale over a longer stretch, and a large part of it is probably the caffeine that is also in those products. A cup of green tea with a meal? Fine, a healthy habit, no problem. A concentrated extract in a capsule that you pay for as a "fat burner"? You are wasting money. High-dose EGCG extracts also have reported cases of liver strain, so more is definitely not better.

Nicotine and ephedrine: they work, and that is why you should leave them

This part is different from the rest of the article. Ephedrine (and the old "ECA" stack with caffeine and aspirin) and nicotine really do suppress appetite and raise expenditure. I will not lie to you. The problem is the price you pay for it.

Ephedrine drives the heart, raises blood pressure, wrecks sleep, creates anxiety, and is linked with serious cardiovascular events. That is why it is not sold over the counter as a "fat burner," and there is a reason: people died on it. Nicotine is strongly addictive, and buying a dependence for a mild drop in appetite is the worst trade you can make. These are not fat loss tools. They are risks dressed up as a shortcut. Leave them.

And now the marketing story

Here is the gallery of things sold with a beautiful label that, in blinded tests against placebo, mostly fail. No drama. They just do not work the way they promise:

SupplementWhat it promisesThe reality
L-carnitineIt carries fat into the cell, where it gets burned. More carnitine means more burning.A healthy body has enough carnitine, and adding more does not kick it into gear. It does not reduce fat in ordinary people.
CLAConjugated linoleic acid "melts" fat and builds muscle.It showed something in animals. In humans the results are tiny to nonexistent. A marketing legend.
Raspberry ketonesWeight loss like the one in the television ad.Hype from one talk show. No proper human trial has ever confirmed it.
Garcinia cambogiaHCA blocks fat production and blunts appetite.The effect on weight is zero to negligible, and there are reported cases of liver damage.
"Detox" or "metabolism booster" teasThey clean you out, start your metabolism, and melt fat.Mostly just diuretic and laxative herbs. You lose water, not fat. And it is back in the morning.

The common thread? Either it was never properly tested in people, or it was tested and the difference is one you cannot see. What "works" on a detox tea is usually water loss and an emptied gut. The scale drops the next day. Fat is not involved at all. It is an illusion you pay for every month.

Why even the things that "work" are not enough

Say you take caffeine and green tea and both agree with you. What do you actually have? A small effect, and mostly a short one. The body gets used to stimulants, the early bump in expenditure fades, and you are left where something else decides the result: how much you eat, and how much you move.

This is the core of the fat burner problem. People take them as a substitute for the work, not as a cherry on top. They buy the capsules and tick "I am doing something about it." At best, that capsule is a one percent help for performance. The other 99 percent is food and the deficit, and nobody can buy that for you. A supplement can be a period at the end of a well-set base. It never replaces the base.

Safety: underestimate this at your own risk

Commercial "fat burners" often mix large doses of stimulants (caffeine, synephrine, yohimbine, and others), and here it is no longer about wasted money. It is about health. What that can do:

  • Heart and blood pressure. Pounding, a fast pulse, higher blood pressure. If you have any cardiovascular problem, this is outright dangerous.
  • Sleep. A stimulant in the afternoon breaks your sleep, and poor sleep reliably slows fat loss. You are shooting yourself in the foot.
  • Anxiety and jitteriness. Large doses of stimulants cause shakiness and inner restlessness. It does not suit everyone.
  • The liver. High-dose extracts (green tea, garcinia) have reported cases of liver strain in the literature. That is not a joke.

And because supplements are not regulated the way drugs are, you often do not know exactly what is in the capsule, or at what dose. So for anyone on medication, or with a health problem, the rule is simple: ask your doctor first, and only then swallow anything.

What to do with this

Let us turn that into a decision you can make today:

  • Skip these outright: L-carnitine, CLA, raspberry ketones, garcinia, "detox" and "metabolism booster" teas, commercial fat burners with a secret stimulant blend. Wasted money, and sometimes a risk.
  • At most, consider this: ordinary caffeine, used on purpose before training (even just coffee), if it helps you train better. Green tea as a drink, not as an expensive extract. Do not expect more than that.
  • Where 90 percent of the result sits: a modest calorie deficit, enough protein (roughly 1.6 to 2.2 g per kilogram), strength training, steps, and sleep. That is what burns fat. Nobody sells it in a capsule, which is exactly why the ads do not talk about it.

Next time you see "fat burner" on a shelf, translate the label as "a supplement that will not eat less for you." Because that is the whole truth. Be Effective!
